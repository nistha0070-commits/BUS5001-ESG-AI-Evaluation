# BUS5001 Q4 – Evaluation of Google NotebookLM

## Project Overview

This project evaluates Google NotebookLM as an AI-powered academic assistant for university students studying Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) reporting standards.

The investigation assesses its suitability for document analysis, academic revision, formative assessment and multimodal learning.

## Academic Scenario

A university student preparing for an ESG reporting examination uses NotebookLM to analyse two official Global Reporting Initiative publications:

- GRI 303: Water and Effluents 2018
- GRI 306: Waste 2020

Both PDFs were uploaded into NotebookLM as reference sources.

## Experiments Conducted

### Experiment 1 – Document Comparison and Citation Verification

NotebookLM generated a structured comparison of both GRI standards, covering reporting scope, environmental impacts, measurement units and disclosure requirements.

The generated response included numbered source citations. A selected citation opened a relevant passage in the GRI 306 document, demonstrating source traceability.

However, not every generated claim was independently verified.

### Experiment 2 – Academic Study Guide

NotebookLM produced learning objectives, explanations of important concepts, disclosure summaries, workplace examples and revision questions.

The study guide supported the organisation of complex academic material. Nevertheless, condensed AI-generated explanations can omit reporting conditions or simplify technical requirements.

### Experiment 3 – Interactive Quiz

NotebookLM generated a 12-question sustainability quiz.

One observed question assessed the freshwater classification threshold under GRI 303. The generated feedback correctly identified the relevant threshold as 1,000 mg/L total dissolved solids.

Only one question was examined in detail, so the accuracy of the full quiz was not established.

### Experiment 4 – Mind Map

NotebookLM generated a visual comparison with separate branches for GRI 303, GRI 306 and their principal differences.

The displayed branches correctly grouped management disclosures 303-1 to 303-2 and 306-1 to 306-2, alongside topic disclosures 303-3 to 303-5 and 306-3 to 306-5.

The mind map supported conceptual understanding, although detailed calculations and reporting exceptions require consultation of the original standards.

### Experiment 5 – Audio Overview

NotebookLM generated a 5-minute, 39-second audio discussion using the uploaded source documents.

The recording could be played through the NotebookLM interface, demonstrating its potential usefulness for listening-based revision.

The screenshot confirms audio generation and playback but does not independently establish the accuracy of its spoken content.

## Overall Evaluation

NotebookLM demonstrated useful capabilities for source-based academic research, document comparison, study preparation and alternative learning formats.

Its main advantage was the ability to transform lengthy technical documents into accessible learning resources while providing links to supporting source passages.

However, students must verify important claims against primary sources, particularly when working with regulatory and reporting standards.

## Recommendation

NotebookLM is suitable for a limited university pilot as a supplementary learning assistant, subject to academic integrity guidance, student training, data-protection controls and independent verification of consequential claims.

It should not replace original readings, lecturer instruction or independent academic judgement.
